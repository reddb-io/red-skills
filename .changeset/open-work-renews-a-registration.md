Open work now renews a project's registration, so a drain no longer stops on its own within one window. ADR 0130 Amendment 4 made the registration the thing that keeps a drain alive and gave it a deadline; the deadline shipped and the renewal had no owner — `project-renew` existed on the daemon, was authorized, and was called by nothing — so a project registered at 22:19 with a `renew_by` of 22:26 was gone minutes later, no Worker could be born after that, and nothing said so. The owner is now the daemon, which is the one party that outlives the session: every read of the registration set holds up, at that instant, each project it can see work for — a counted, positive queue depth from its own poll, or a Worker it is itself holding — and lapses whatever is left. A counted zero sustains nothing, so a project that has finished still lapses on schedule and stops being polled; an uncountable queue sustains nothing either, because that is exactly the silence a closed laptop makes. The session's own clock is untouched, so the three states stay distinguishable: `renewing` is a live session, `self-renewing` is the daemon holding a registration up on the project's own work, and `running-on` is work nobody is watching on a deadline it will lapse at. The failure also stopped hiding: a lapse is kept as a stated fact with its instant and its reason instead of being reported as an absence, and a project the host knows only because a Worker carries its label is now rendered as known **by name** rather than as a registered, healthy project.
