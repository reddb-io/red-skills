The host daemon has a window: `apps/vscode-redskilled/` is a VSCode extension that reads `redskilled` and shows it where the work happens — a tree of live Workers with their vitals, a panel that follows one Worker's log, the host event lane, each registered project's open pull requests, and a notification when any of it changes. It **reads and never writes**, because ADR 0130 rule 9 makes reach asymmetric and a view that could stop another project's Worker would be a control surface wearing a monitor's name; it also never spawns the daemon, so a tree restoring with a window is not what births a machine-wide singleton. The socket path, the lane decoder and the protocol types are IMPORTED from `@reddb-io/redskilled` rather than mirrored, so the extension cannot drift into a private idea of what a socket or a lane row is — and that is why the wire is TOON end to end (`resident-wire` rules 1–3, #2947/#2948) with no `JSON.parse` anywhere near a frame. Notifications fire on a **transition** and never on a state: the first read of a session is silent, so is the first read after an outage, and each kind has a renotify window, because the alternative is that the loudest moment of the day is a wall of facts that had not changed. Nothing under `src/model`, `src/watch` or `src/redskilled` imports `vscode`, so the whole of the layout, the transition detection and the log following is driven by 59 tests against a fake daemon on a real unix socket writing real TOONL through the daemon's own lane writer. The installable `.vsix` is built by a hundred lines of OPC zip in `src/packaging/vsix.ts` rather than by `@vscode/vsce` — 290 packages and 130 MB in every CI install, to produce an archive nothing publishes — and it is deterministic, so two builds of one tree are byte-identical.
